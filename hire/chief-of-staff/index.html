Hire a Chief of Staff who extends the founder.

The right Chief of Staff gives a founder back ten hours a week and makes the whole leadership team run better. The wrong one becomes an expensive note-taker. The difference is almost always in how the role is defined before the search starts.

What the role is, and what it is not

A startup Chief of Staff owns the founder's highest-leverage unowned problems. In practice that is some mix of running the operating cadence (planning, weekly metrics, board prep), driving cross-functional projects nobody else has the bandwidth for, and acting as a thinking partner on decisions. The best ones are judged on outcomes the founder would otherwise have had to deliver personally.

It is not an executive assistant role, and it is not a permanent position for most people. Strong Chiefs of Staff typically rotate into a line role (operations, product, a GM seat) after 18 to 36 months. Saying this out loud in the job description attracts better candidates, not worse ones.

Before you open the search, write down the three problems this person will own in their first six months. If you cannot, you are not ready to hire a Chief of Staff; you may need an operations lead or an EA instead.

When to make this hire

  • The founder is the bottleneck on more than two important workstreams at once.
  • The leadership team has grown past five or six and the operating rhythm is slipping.
  • A fundraise, a major launch or a reorganization is coming and nobody owns the program.
  • The founder wants a successor pipeline for a future ops or GM role.

Profiles that work

  • Former consultants and bankers two to five years in, who want to build rather than advise. Strong on structure and analysis; check for comfort with ambiguity and hands-on execution.
  • Early startup operators who have been a BizOps or strategy generalist at a company one stage ahead of yours. Often the fastest ramp.
  • Ex-founders whose company did not make it. Excellent judgment and urgency; make sure they are ready to work inside someone else's vision.
  • Internal promotions from a strong analyst or product ops seat. Lowest risk on culture, highest risk on seniority with the leadership team.

How to interview for judgment

  1. Founder conversation (60 min). Walk through a real decision you are facing right now. Do they ask good questions, structure the problem, and push back when they disagree?
  2. Written exercise. Give them a messy internal document (a board deck draft, a planning memo) and ask for a one-page rewrite plus the three questions they would ask the team. Writing quality predicts this role well.
  3. Leadership team interviews. Two or three execs who will work with them weekly. You are testing whether they can hold their own without a title to lean on.
  4. Reference calls you run yourself. Ask the former manager what the candidate owned end to end and what they would not trust them with yet.

Mistakes we see most

  • Hiring for pedigree instead of ownership. A great resume with no end-to-end delivery is a slow ramp.
  • Leaving the scope open and letting the role fill with admin work. Protect it in the first 90 days.
  • Underpaying relative to the operator market and then losing the candidate to a BizOps offer with equity.
  • Skipping the exit conversation. Candidates want to know what this leads to; tell them.

Common questions

What does a startup Chief of Staff do?

They own the founder's most important unowned problems: the operating cadence, cross-functional projects, board and investor preparation, and acting as a thinking partner on decisions. The scope should be written down before the search begins.

How senior should a Chief of Staff be at a Series A?

Typically three to seven years of experience, often from consulting, banking, a startup BizOps seat, or a prior founding role. Seniority matters less than demonstrated end-to-end ownership and the ability to work with executives without a formal title.

Is Chief of Staff the same as BizOps?

They overlap. A Chief of Staff is attached to one leader and their priorities. A BizOps hire is attached to the business and owns systems, metrics and planning across functions. Early-stage startups often start with one person doing both and split the roles later.

How long do Chiefs of Staff stay in the role?

Eighteen to thirty-six months is typical before moving into an operations, product or GM role. The best candidates expect this and want to hear what the path looks like.

Do you place Chiefs of Staff outside San Francisco and New York?

Yes, though most of our searches are in those two markets because that is where the companies and the candidate pool are concentrated. We also work with teams in Seattle, Los Angeles and Austin.
