Operations

Hire a Founding BizOps lead who builds the machine.

Your first operations hire sets up the systems, metrics and plans the company will run on for years. It is one of the highest-leverage seats in an early-stage company and one of the easiest to under-hire.

What a founding BizOps hire owns

At seed through Series B, BizOps is the generalist who takes whatever is most broken or most important and fixes it. In the first year that usually means standing up the metrics stack and a weekly business review, building the financial model and hiring plan, running pricing and packaging work, owning vendor and tooling decisions, and taking the lead on one or two strategic projects such as a new market or a partnership.

As the company grows, the role either specializes (finance, revenue operations, product operations) or the person becomes the leader of a BizOps team. Strong candidates want to know which path you have in mind.

The best founding BizOps hires are measured on decisions made faster and with better data, not on decks produced.

When to make this hire

  • The founders are still the only people who can answer basic questions about the business with numbers.
  • Planning happens in a founder's head and gets communicated inconsistently.
  • There are three or more important projects (pricing, a new segment, a partnership) that keep slipping because nobody owns them.
  • You are preparing for a raise and the data room would take weeks to assemble.

Profiles that work

  • Consultants and bankers with two to five years of experience who have already done one stint inside a company, or who show clear evidence of hands-on building outside their day job.
  • Operators from a startup one stage ahead who were an early BizOps, strategy or finance hire and want to do it again with more ownership.
  • Product or growth analysts with strong SQL and modeling who have shown they can run a project, not only analyze it.
  • Ex-founders who have run a small P&L and know what it feels like when the numbers are wrong.

How to interview

  1. Business deep dive (60 min). Share your real metrics. Ask what they would want to know next and why. You are testing curiosity and business sense.
  2. Modeling or data exercise. A small, realistic take-home: build a simple model or analysis from messy data and write up the recommendation. Judge clarity of thought over spreadsheet tricks.
  3. Project retrospective. Walk through a cross-functional project they owned end to end. Who resisted, what broke, what they would do differently.
  4. Founder fit. This person will be in most important meetings. Make sure you want them there.

Mistakes we see most

  • Titling the role Chief of Staff when the work is BizOps, or the reverse. Candidates self-select on the title; the wrong one shrinks your pool.
  • Hiring someone too junior to push back on the founders, which turns the seat into an analyst role.
  • Demanding a specific prior industry. Operating skill transfers; domain knowledge is learnable in a quarter.
  • Letting the process drag. Strong BizOps candidates in San Francisco routinely hold two or three offers.

Common questions

What is the difference between BizOps and Chief of Staff?

BizOps owns the business: metrics, planning, systems and strategic projects across functions. A Chief of Staff owns one leader's priorities. At the earliest stage one person often does both; by Series B they are usually separate seats.

How much experience should a founding BizOps hire have?

Usually two to six years. Enough to have owned real projects end to end, not so much that they expect a team on day one. Judgment and ownership matter more than the year count.

Should founding BizOps report to the CEO or the COO?

At seed and Series A, almost always the CEO. The role depends on access to decisions and context. Once there is a COO or CFO, it typically moves under them.

Do BizOps candidates need technical skills?

Strong SQL and spreadsheet modeling are the baseline. Comfort with your data tools and the ability to prototype with light scripting is a real advantage at an AI company, but you are not hiring an engineer.

How long does a Founding BizOps search take?

Three to five weeks with a tight loop is typical. One Series B client sent an offer letter 24 days after our first submissions.
